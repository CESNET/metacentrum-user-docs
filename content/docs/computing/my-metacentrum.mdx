**[myMetaCentrum](https://my.metacentrum.cz)** is a web application with live data about your jobs, the queues, the machines, and your storage quotas on MetaCentrum. The data is refreshed automatically (the freshness of each data source is shown under the menu).

You can use it to:

- watch your **running and queued jobs** and inspect how efficiently they ran,
- check which **machines and queues** are free before you submit,
- build a syntactically correct **`qsub` command** with the Qsub assembler,
- review your **storage quotas** and free space on `/storage` disks,
- list your **cloud projects**, **group members**, and **groups**.


## Personal View

The landing page summarizes your current state: jobs queued, running, and finished, CPU and GPU utilization, and a list of your recent PBS jobs. Click the **user button** with your login to open the user detail, which shows your membership expiration, fairshare ranking per scheduler server, and yearly usage statistics.

## Qsub assembler

The [Qsub assembler](https://my.metacentrum.cz/qsub-assembler) is an interactive tool for building a syntactically correct `qsub` command. Select the resources your job needs — walltime, queue, CPU count, RAM, GPU count, scratch type and size, cluster, and advanced properties such as CPU vendor or GPU capabilities — and click **Show Results**.

![pic](/img/meta/computing/mymeta-qsubass-form.png)

You get the parameters in two forms: a `qsub` one-liner for the command line, and `#PBS` directives for pasting into a batch script.

```bash
qsub -l walltime=01:00:00 -q gpu@pbs-m1.metacentrum.cz -l select=1:ncpus=1:mem=1gb:ngpus=1:scratch_local=400mb
```

![pic](/img/meta/computing/mymeta-qsubass-results.png)

Below the command, the assembler shows **how many machines match your criteria and how busy they are right now** (free, partially free, or occupied), so you can estimate the waiting time before submitting.

## Machines

Browse, list, and inspect all compute nodes. Filter the list by node **state** (free, partially free, occupied, maintenance), by **queue**, **cluster**, or **resource**. The overview shows totals for organizations, clusters, and nodes.

![pic](/img/meta/computing/mymeta-machines.png)

Clicking a machine opens its detail — CPU model and count, memory, GPUs, local scratch type and free space, and current state.

![pic](/img/meta/computing/mymeta-machine-detail.png)

## Storage spaces

The *Storage Spaces* page has two parts:

* **My Storage Quotas** — your used space, soft/hard quotas, and file counts in every home directory.

![pic](/img/meta/computing/mymeta-storage-1.png)

* **Disk Arrays** — total capacity and free space of the shared `/storage` disks, plus scratch storage notes (data on node scratch is deleted 14 days after the job ends).

![pic](/img/meta/computing/mymeta-storage-2.png)

## Cloud Projects

Lists your virtual-infrastructure projects with their reserved resources; each project has a detail page.

## Jobs Queues

The *Jobs Queues* page lists all queues with their properties — priority, time limits, and how many jobs are currently queued and running.

![pic](/img/meta/computing/mymeta-queues.png)

The queue detail shows its limits and the jobs waiting in it, so you can estimate when yours will start.

![pic](/img/meta/computing/mymeta-queues-2.png)

## Jobs

The *Jobs* page has three tabs: **My Jobs**, **All Jobs**, and **Waiting Jobs**.

![pic](/img/meta/computing/mymeta-jobs.png)

In *All Jobs* you see every job currently running or waiting on the PBS servers. Other users' jobs are displayed as **"Anonym"** — a GDPR measure; you cannot see their real names or usernames.

Clicking a job opens its detail: basic information, resources reserved vs. actually used, and **efficiency messages** that tell you how to tune the job, for example:

> CPU usage is below 75 % (0 %). The job was inefficient. Consider reducing the number of CPUs requested.
>
> Job finished way sooner than requested. Consider reducing the requested walltime.
>
> Memory usage is below 75 % (14 %). Consider reducing the amount of memory requested.

![pic](/img/meta/computing/mymeta-jobs-2.png)

## Users

Under *Users* you can list all users who share a UNIX group with you — in other words, your collaborators.

![pic](/img/meta/computing/mymeta-users.png)

## Groups

Similarly, the *Groups* page lists all groups you are a member of, with their GID and member count.
